  1. The Ballot or the Bullet: Political Inclusion, Ethnic Fractionalization and Civil Conflict

    选票抑或子弹：政治吸纳、族群分化与国内冲突

    Han Donglin and Luo Zeyang

    World Economics and Politics (世界经济与政治), 2025(10), 87–119. In Chinese.

    PDF ↗
    Show abstract / 查看摘要Hide abstract / 收起摘要

    Abstract

    Political inclusion, as an institutional factor, influences civil conflict by shaping the motives and capabilities of political elites. Specifically, political inclusion can be divided into parliamentary inclusion and governmental inclusion at the theoretical level, which incorporate political elites into different political spaces, differentially reshape their motives and capabilities, and ultimately exert varying impacts on civil conflict. Ethnic fractionalization plays a moderating role in the relationship between political inclusion and civil conflict, with political inclusion showing distinct effects on civil conflict under different ethnic fractionalization thresholds. To validate this framework, the authors utilize data from 156 countries from 1981 to 2018, employing multiple regression models to empirically test the differences between parliamentary inclusion and governmental inclusion, and reveal the nonlinear effects of ethnic fractionalization in the relationship between political inclusion and civil conflict through threshold regression models and moderation effect models. Meanwhile, case studies on Ghana, Yemen, and Nigeria reveal the specific mechanism through which political inclusion affects civil conflict. This study expands the theoretical understanding of political inclusion and provides a new perspective for comprehending the complex relationship between political inclusion and civil conflict.

    中文摘要

    政治吸纳作为一种制度性因素，通过塑造政治精英的动机和能力对国内冲突产生影响。具体而言，政治吸纳在理论层面可分为议会吸纳和政府吸纳，二者将政治精英吸纳进不同的政治空间，差异化地重塑其动机和能力，最终对国内冲突产生不同的影响。族群分化在政治吸纳和国内冲突的关系中发挥着调节作用，在不同的族群分化阈值下，政治吸纳对国内冲突的影响表现出明显差异。为验证这一分析框架，作者基于1981—2018年156个国家的数据，采用多种回归模型实证检验议会吸纳和政府吸纳的差异，并通过门槛回归模型和调节效应模型揭示了族群分化在政治吸纳和国内冲突关系中的非线性影响，同时以加纳、也门和尼日利亚为案例剖析政治吸纳影响国内冲突的具体机制路径。该研究结果一定程度上拓展了对政治吸纳的理论认知，为理解政治吸纳和国内冲突的复杂关系提供了新视角。

  1. The Colonial Origins of Urban Conflict in Africa: Evidence from Spatial Analysis

    非洲城市冲突的殖民起源——来自空间分析的证据

    Luo Zeyang and Han Donglin

    PDF ↗
    Show abstract / 查看摘要Hide abstract / 收起摘要

    Abstract

    Cities are generally regarded as important foundations of political stability. In Africa, however, a striking anomaly emerges: rapid urbanization has not brought widespread political stability, and urban areas have instead experienced extensive civil conflict. This paper argues that the key to understanding this anomaly lies in the colonial origins of African cities. To a large extent, African cities originated as spatial nodes established by colonial powers to extract resources and control society. Colonial powers pursued two principal activities at these nodes. First, material extraction through railway construction, resource exploitation, and related activities promoted the concentration of resources in urban nodes. Second, colonial control through religious proselytization, access to education, and related practices produced persistent social differentiation within cities. Through path dependence, resource concentration and social differentiation became entrenched as structural features of contemporary African cities. Resource concentration increases the returns to conflict, while social differentiation provides a basis for conflict mobilization; together, they increase the risk of civil conflict. Using spatial panel data covering 10,652 grid cells in Africa from 1992 to 2010, this paper tests the argument with colonial railways and mission stations as proxies for colonial legacies while accounting for spatial dependence. The analysis finds a positive association between the distribution of urban settlements and civil conflict, which colonial legacies further strengthen. Mechanism tests further indicate that colonial railways and mission stations increase the risk of civil conflict in urban areas by promoting resource concentration and reinforcing ethnic exclusion, respectively. This paper provides a historical explanation and fine-grained empirical evidence for the relationship between cities and civil conflict in Africa.

    中文摘要

    城市通常被视作政治稳定的重要载体，但在非洲地区却呈现出明显的异常：快速的城市化并未带来广泛的政治稳定，反而在城市地区爆发了大量的国内冲突。本文认为，理解这一异常的关键在于非洲城市的殖民起源。非洲城市在很大程度上是殖民者为攫取资源和控制社会而创建的空间节点，殖民者在这些节点上主要开展两种核心活动：其一是通过修建铁路、资源开采等方式实现物质攫取，推动资源在城市节点高度集聚；其二是通过宗教传播、教育准入等手段实施殖民控制，在城市内部造成持续的社会分化。资源集聚与社会分化因路径依赖而不断固化，成为当代非洲城市的结构性特征。资源集聚提高了冲突行动的收益，社会分化则为冲突动员提供了基础，二者共同增加了国内冲突的爆发风险。本文利用1992—2010年非洲10,652个单元格的空间面板数据，以殖民铁路和传教站作为殖民历史遗产的代理变量，在控制空间依赖的基础上进行实证检验。分析发现，城市分布与国内冲突呈现正相关，而殖民历史遗产进一步强化了这一关系。机制检验进一步表明，殖民铁路和传教站分别通过促进资源集聚和强化族群排斥，增加了城市地区的国内冲突风险。本文为非洲城市与国内冲突的关系提供了历史性解释和细颗粒度的经验证据。

  2. Backfire of the Dragon’s Gift: Does Chinese Aid Erode the World Bank’s Peace Dividend?

    Luo Zeyang

    Slides ↗
    Show abstractHide abstract

    Abstract

    This paper examines whether Chinese aid changes the peacebuilding effects of World Bank aid in post-conflict Africa. Using geocoded aid and conflict data for 327 provinces in 37 African countries from 1992 to 2014, we estimate stratified Cox models of recurrent conflict with a gap-time specification. World Bank aid is associated with a lower hazard of conflict recurrence, but this association is substantially weaker where Chinese aid is also present. Chinese aid alone shows no clear association with recurrence. The main pattern persists across alternative aid measures, conflict datasets, matching procedures, placebo tests, and instrumental-variable checks. Mechanism analyses link Chinese aid to lower government compliance with World Bank project agreements and poorer project performance; the peace-sustaining association of World Bank aid is weaker under these conditions. The attenuation is particularly pronounced for Chinese infrastructure aid and in more corrupt institutional environments. These findings indicate that the peacebuilding effects of aid depend on interactions among donors and on the institutional conditions governing aid implementation.

  3. Journey to the West: Oversea Education and Procedural Internal Governance in Rebel Organizations

    Luo Zeyang and Su Yusung

    Show abstractHide abstract

    Abstract

    Why do some rebel organizations establish more extensive formal rules and procedures for governing their members? This paper examines how leaders’ overseas education shapes procedural internal governance: the codification of behavioral expectations and the establishment of formal procedures for regulating conduct, investigating violations, and adjudicating internal disputes. We argue that studying abroad exposes future leaders to unfamiliar institutions and encourages comparisons of organizational practices, increasing their willingness to adopt procedural arrangements. Translating this motivation into organizational rules also depends on the capacity of central command to implement leadership decisions. Combining leadership biographies, institutional records, and conflict data, we construct a panel of 138 rebel organizations from 1990 to 2012. Organizations led by individuals who studied abroad exhibit more extensive procedural governance. This relationship persists across alternative specifications and sensitivity checks, with instrumental-variable estimates consistent with the baseline findings. Comparisons with overseas employment and exile reveal a stronger association for education, while the association is weaker where leaders have limited control over subordinate units. The findings identify leaders’ formative experiences as a micro-foundation of institutional development within armed organizations.
